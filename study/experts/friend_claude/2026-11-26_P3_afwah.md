3.2 Denial wording vs truth

Wording	Cases	What followed
"Declined to comment" / no response	Broadcom, Intel, Huang's representative, Semafor sources, WSJ oust report	4 T, 1 P: weakly bullish for the rumour
Categorical denial of talks, filed with the exchange	Jio Financial, Paytm	Held (F)

Reassurance ("strong capital")	Credit Suisse	Collapsed 5.5 months later
"Hacked / bogus / unauthorized", from the account owner via a second channel	AP, SEC, Lilly	Reliable (F)

The rule: give the most weight to a denial of fact, from the party that controls the outcome, filed where it carries legal liability. A denial of current intent or a reassurance carries little weight. Check whether the other party also denies.

3.4 Red flags for planted or fake rumours

A new or paid-verified handle (Lilly).
A hacked official account. Check the organisation's website or a second channel (AP, SEC).
An aggregator headline with no primary link (Hassett).
Tip groups claiming credentials they don't have. The bullrun2017 channel claimed to be run by analysts with 40 years' combined experience who were "getting" SEBI registration. 
barandbench
Illiquid small caps, round-number price targets, urgency.
A disclosed short position: Hindenburg said it had shorted Adani stocks. That doesn't make a report false, but discount it.

3.5 Price pattern

For true rumours that were denied (Zee, Nvidia return, Yes Bank's moratorium), the confirmation day moved 3–10× more than the rumour day. The market underprices denied rumours.
For hyped, widely expected events, gains faded after confirmation (Nvidia -8% two days later).

0–34: ignore.
35–54: watch. Set alerts and check filings.
55–74: small position with a hard stop, or defined-risk options. Risk no more than 0.5–1% of capital.
75+: strong. Still size it, and expect confirmation or a kill soon.
