1. The AI Bubble Already Popped | TCAF 269 (The Compound)

Hosts: Josh Brown and Michael Batnick. Guests: Jenny Harrington (Gilman Hill Asset Management) and Bryn Talkington (Requisite Capital).

Was there an AI bubble, and has it popped?

Josh says the AI bubble popped back in July. Dell, Micron and the DRAM/memory ETF have each roughly halved, and leveraged tech ETF assets collapsed from about $130B to about $50B.
The others push back. In their view, the market's mood about AI deflated, but most valuations were never bubbly: Micron traded at about 3–7x earnings and Nvidia at about 12x forward earnings versus about 20x for the market. Data-center building is still going on.
Bryn's view: if hyperscaler capex slows and free cash flow turns positive again, that could actually help the market, since the Mag 7 are about 38% of it.
The Nasdaq is about 15% off its high, while equal-weight stocks and real-economy names (the "HALO" theme) have held up. They compare this to the 2000 split between the "Berkshire market" and the "Intel market."

Nvidia

Nvidia just authorised a $350B buyback, about 9% of its market cap.
Jensen Huang's departure is called a "thesis violation." The open questions are whether leadership knew chips were reaching blacklisted Chinese firms, and how much China revenue is at risk.
Personal allegations about him also come up in the banter. These are claims made on the show, not established facts.
Jenny's framework, from Bruce Greenwald: your starting point is always today, and after a drawdown you rewrite the investment thesis. Her point is that Nvidia's thesis can't be rewritten yet because there are too many unknowns.
Buyers of computing power are said to have gained leverage. One point made: AMD chips now take days rather than months to code for, which weakens Nvidia's CUDA advantage.

Anthropic IPO (on the day of recording)

It raised about $81B, the largest IPO ever, ahead of SpaceX at about $75B and SK Hynix at $26.5B.
It priced at $83 and was trading around $97 that day. Retail investors got about 20% of the allocation (versus 30% for SpaceX).
Demand data cited: customers spending over $100K a year grew from about 1,500 to about 11,900 in a year.
They warn that big IPOs usually fall 30–50% at some point in their first year. Examples given: SpaceX, SK Hynix, Alibaba, CoreWeave, Rivian and Blackstone.
Jenny's analogy: the labs are all building Ferraris, but most users need a CR-V. Tools that route each task to the right model will decide how much companies spend.
The group criticises Rep. Pramila Jayapal's statement opposing the IPO.

Other topics

Wall Street is promoting IPOs with unusual openness.
Private-market valuations look inflated. Kalshi is reportedly raising at $60–65B, versus Robinhood's roughly $60B market cap, while Michigan has ordered it to shut down in the state.
Daily sector rotation is unusually violent. They blame algorithmic trading reacting to tweets and sector indices dominated by two or three stocks.

2. How to Find Stocks Where FII & DII Holdings Are Rising (Groww)
India outperformed global markets until 2023 but has lagged since 2024. In October 2026 the Nifty was about 12% below its all-time high, because of heavy foreign-investor (FII) selling and high crude oil prices.
It then rose about 9% in the first week of November.
The suggested screener uses three filters:
foreign-investor (FII) holding up more than 1%
domestic-institution (DII) holding up more than 1%
market cap above ₹3,000 Cr (or whatever range you prefer)
The video warns that rising holdings alone are not a buy signal. You should still check growth, debt, cash flow and valuation.

4. Jio Platforms IPO Review 2026 (Asset Yogi)

IPO details

Price band ₹1,065–1,119, lots of 12 shares, and a quota for existing (Reliance) shareholders.
Post-issue market cap about ₹10.3 lakh Cr. It's expected to join the Nifty 50 soon.
Subscription ran 21–23 October 2026, with listing expected on 28 October. That date has passed, so it should already be trading.
The video gives the issue size as about ₹30,000 Cr. Reliance owned about 66% before the IPO.
About ₹27,500 Cr of the proceeds will repay debt; none goes to growth.

Business

About 53.3 crore customers, up 7.1%. Market share is about 50% in wireless broadband and 43% in fixed broadband. About 53% of customers are on 5G.
Average revenue per user (ARPU) is ₹215.6, about 17% below Airtel's ₹257.2, and it grew only 3.3% last quarter.
Revenue: 77% from prepaid plans. Reliance Retail is the only distributor of prepaid plans.

Financials

Revenue grew from about ₹1.1 lakh Cr in FY24 to ₹1.5 lakh Cr in FY26 (16% CAGR), but only 12.6% in the latest year.
Operating margin is about 50–52%. Profit grew from about ₹21,000 Cr to about ₹30,000 Cr, but growth slowed to 15% in FY26 as finance costs doubled to about ₹8,600 Cr.
Network spending fell from ₹53,000 Cr to ₹34,200 Cr. About ₹1 lakh Cr of spectrum payments are still owed to the government and are not counted in debt.

Valuation: about 34x earnings versus Airtel's 37x. Airtel's 3-year earnings growth is about 40% against Jio's about 18%, so the video calls the discount fair rather than a bargain. It says the price came in about 30% below what was expected.

Risks

Growth depends on tariff hikes, and the timing of the next one is uncertain.
Spectrum dues plus the expected 2030 6G auction mean continued borrowing.
Heavy dealings with related parties, and Reliance-group broadband companies (GTPL, Hathway, DEN) compete with Jio.
Starlink satellite broadband is ready to launch pending approval, while Jio has no satellite timeline.

Verdict: worth applying for listing gains, but not large ones. Long-term holders should keep these risks in mind.
