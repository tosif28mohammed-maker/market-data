1. Bollinger Band Edge Most Traders Fail to Discover (Trading Chanakya, qlhjUR62fpU)
This is a long-only strategy for index ETFs that combines Bollinger Band breakouts with William O'Neil-style relative strength (RS).

Filter: Use a sheet of 34 indices. Keep only indices with an RS rating of 70 or more (for individual stocks the threshold is 80–90 or more), trading above the 50-day level and close to their 52-week high. Skip anything 8–20% or more below its high. Examples he gives: Pharma, Healthcare, Private Bank.
Entry: A candle closes above the upper band, and you buy when price breaks that candle's high.
Exit: Stop-loss 1% below that candle's low. Target is 3× the stop distance.
Portfolio rules: Hold at most 3–4 ETFs, with no more than 2 from the same industry. Risk about 1% of capital per trade (for example, ₹5,000 on ₹5 lakh).
Claimed backtest (2015–2026): Buy-and-hold had about a 40% drawdown and about 21% CAGR. The strategy had about a 12% drawdown and 20.5–24% CAGR, with only 1–2 negative years. He claims ₹10L grew to about ₹1.4Cr before short-term capital gains tax, and says the 3-ETF version did best.
Alternative without trading: Each month, hold the top 4 ETFs by RS and replace any that drop out of the top.
He promotes the sheet through his Telegram channel.

5. Save Future Trading Cost Using Synthetic Options (Trading Chanakya, trGsEk7_MR4)
This video shows how to replace Nifty futures with synthetic futures to cut costs.

The cost problem: One Nifty futures round trip costs about ₹1,000, mostly the STT charged on the sell side, which was recently raised.
Synthetic long: Buy an ATM call and sell a put at the same strike and expiry. To go short, buy the put and sell the call instead. With a lot size of 65, it moves like a future: a 100-point move gives about ±₹6,500.
Cost of the synthetic: About ₹140 per round trip for both legs. Up to ₹80 of that is brokerage at ₹20 per order. With a ₹5–10 per order broker it drops to about ₹80–100, and the floor is about ₹60 if brokerage is zero.
Caveats:
It suits intraday or 1–2 day trades. For longer holds, a regular future is the better choice.
Costs rise early in the expiry week, when option premiums are higher.
Stop-losses are harder because there are two legs. You need P&L-based exits (he uses the Fyers platform) or alerts on the Nifty chart.
STT can be offset against profits, but that doesn't help when you're in a loss.
