I've summarized all 9 transcripts below. Most are in Hindi or Hinglish; the Asset Yogi and Delta D3 ones are in English. Anything dated after mid-2026 (Warsh as Fed chair, Jensen Huang stepping down, new UPI charges) is reported as the video states it. I haven't checked those claims.

1. Cem Karsan: "The Fed Is Preparing to Print Again" (Real Vision, hqOKMdbUaKk)

Main claim: Fed Chair Warsh's hawkish tone is "theater" to build credibility. Karsan expects large-scale QE on the long end of the curve starting in Q1, which he calls "banana-republic" money printing.
Why: He says there are no natural buyers for US Treasuries, so the Fed becomes the buyer of last resort, as Japan did. He cites Hank Paulson's April Bloomberg interview warning of a Treasury crisis, plus new laws such as the Strategic Debt Ratio Act and the stablecoin act.
Inflation: He sees it as structural, driven by populism. Millennials have about 45% of the wealth boomers had at the same age, and 35% live with their parents. Add fiscal spending, protectionism and global conflict (Ukraine, Iran/Hormuz), and he compares it to the 1970s.
Portfolios: He argues the 60/40 portfolio only worked because rates fell for 40 years. He says it returned 0% in real terms in 1900–20, 1929–49 and 1962–82, and that its Sharpe ratio (0.37) is barely above the S&P's (0.35).
Advice: Know what you own, and always hold hedges ("keep brakes on the race car"). The clip stops after two of his four "tools".

3. Indian companies' strongest growth in 17 quarters (Angel One, jKzfa6188T8)

Earnings: Citing Motilal Oswal, about 400 companies earned ₹4.53 lakh crore combined profit in Q2 FY27, up 32% from ₹3.44 lakh crore. Sales rose 25% and EBITDA 23%.
Nifty 50: Profits grew 27%, the best in 17 quarters. Large caps grew 31%, mid caps 22% and small caps 32%.
Concentration: Banks and financials, metals, oil and gas, and autos produced 77% of the extra profit.
Estimate revisions: 119 upgrades against 104 downgrades.
Outlook: Growth is expected to slow in H2 FY27, to 11% for the full coverage universe and 18% for the Nifty.
What to watch: H2 growth, the upgrade/downgrade ratio and corporate capex.

4. MTF Moving Average (Trading Chanakya, jQ4CJVMOSXo)

Idea: "Swim with the river." It follows Alexander Elder's rule of checking the next timeframe up, about 5x (weekly for daily trades, daily for hourly).
Indicator: A Pine Script made with Claude for Fyers' Smart Script charts. It shows the 20 EMA on hourly, daily, weekly and monthly charts together.
Rules:
Go long only when the weekly trend is bullish.
Go short only after the daily MA crosses below the weekly MA.
Treat the area between the two MAs as a no-trade zone.
Put stops below the nearby consolidation.
Weakness: It gives false signals in sideways markets.
The host promotes a Fyers account and his Telegram channel.

5. Aswath Damodaran: "Nvidia faces a Jensen Test" (kldDGZ-C0MU)

What happened: News of Jensen Huang's affair (with an alleged China link) broke on October 29, followed by reports of affairs with employees who reported to him. He resigned on November 12, and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO.
Market reaction: Nvidia's market cap fell from about $5.2T to $3.6T by November 19. The rest of the Mag 7 and TSMC were flat.
How much a CEO matters: More when value depends on the company's own decisions rather than macro forces, when moats are weak, at transition points, when there is a lot of "optionality", and in young companies. Damodaran maps CEO types to life-cycle stages, from Visionary to Liquidator.
Valuation: He values Nvidia before the scandal at about $4.85T. Short-term damage alone would mean a 5–10% hit, which makes $3.6T look cheap. A long-term hit, for example 3% lower growth and 6% lower margins, cuts value by about 24%.
Five open questions:
Will Jensen return? (Kalshi puts it at about 10%.)
How good is the successor? He thinks Tim Cook is the wrong fit.
Will Jensen cooperate with whoever replaces him?
How deep is the management bench?
Is the China story real?
Governance lesson: Shareholders need to be able to push back against superstar CEOs, including at Anthropic. A dual-class structure would have made this worse. Gates and Bezos are his examples of founders who made themselves unnecessary.

8. New UPI charges 2026 (Asset Yogi, oJJiIVb2T6o)

The rule: NPCI announced it on September 15, 2026, effective October 15. Merchants pay an MDR of 0.4%, capped at ₹300.
No charge on:
Person-to-person transfers
Transactions up to ₹2,000 (above that, 0.4% applies to the full amount)
Small merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month
Autopay and recurring mandates, including SIPs
Reduced rates:
Essential services (railways, fuel, utilities, school fees and similar) pay a flat ₹5.
Mutual fund and stock payments pay 0.02%, capped at ₹300, borne by the AMC or broker, who may pass it on.
Reach: 96% of transactions by count are exempt, but 67% of the money moved is charged.
Who gets the fee: The customer's bank gets 40%, the merchant's bank 30%, the UPI app 20% and the PSP bank 10%.
Risks: More cash demands from merchants, split payments, and smaller e-commerce discounts.

Expected ranges: 24,800–25,500 this week, 24,500–25,800 for the November series and 24,000–26,000 for December. He has sold a 24,800 put and a 25,500 call.
Margin trick: On the day before expiry he moves his hedge closer, from 700 to 200 points away, which frees about ₹32k of margin per lot.
Caveats: He says not to trade on his view, and mentions Livermore and Leeson. He also promotes his Bengaluru workshop.
