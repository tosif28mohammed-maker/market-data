5. Bias and failure notes
Survivorship and hindsight:
The universe (top 500 by 2020 mcap) is fixed at the start, which is fair.
But every list is sorted by ending return, and "Biggest" also requires beating Nifty TRI.
PEG and Payback use actual future profits (the report says "perfect foresight", p51). The 35 compounders were selected on 17-year returns (the report says "perfect hindsight", p13).
Missing base rates:
The report says 80% of compounders had RoE > 15% and 77% were leaders, but never says what share of all top-500 stocks had those traits.
Without that, the claims are untested. This is the first thing your data can fix.
Losers:
Only one page (p52): 24 destroyers, which tells you little because the period started at the covid low.
All top-10 destroyers were consumer-facing, which cuts against the "prefer B2C" rule.
No failure rates are given for the screens. There's no follow-up on past "Fastest" names (1996–2024 table, p41).
Strong evidence:
The cap-band crossover pattern covers 1,000 stocks, and the report says it repeats "every year".
The 10–15 pt outperformance of winners over benchmarks spans 10 periods.
Weak evidence: buying after crashes (7 stocks, one episode), PSU low PE (one period), the sector calls (stories plus China 2007–11 analogy), and the 35 compounder traits (no control group).
One unusual period: 2020–25 began at a crash low. The report admits valuation "did not matter significantly" (p50).
Where the rules could fail:
Low PE in cyclicals at peak earnings.
PEG with tiny or negative growth, and loss-to-profit turnarounds (the 47–48% buckets).
Rate cycles: not in text.
Liquidity: the report sticks to the top 500 and asks "is the stock reasonably liquid?" (Q#24). Your ~1,850 stocks include much smaller names, so cap at roughly the top 500–1,000 by rank.
Method mismatches with your data:
Years end in March (FY).
Returns include dividends; if your prices exclude them, returns will look lower.
CG Power is excluded only because its 2020 rank was beyond 500.
Earlier studies the report cites:
The 2007 NTD forecast: second trillion expected by 2012 at ~15% CAGR, actually came in 2015 at 10%. The report calls this "directionally right but mathematically wrong" (p10).
The QGLP checklist from the 25th study (2020) is reproduced on p32.
Errors inside the text:
"29 compounders" vs a 30-stock list.
"2002 to 2025" vs 2022–25 PAT CAGR.
Sensex CAGR 17% vs 21%.
Mid→Mega 21 vs 22.
"USD 8 billion" (should be trillion).
PSU PAT CAGR 34% vs 35%; private 26% vs 37%.
