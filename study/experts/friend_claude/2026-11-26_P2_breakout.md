3. fails_when (conditions you can code; test each as a filter on/off)

Index regime: Nifty 500 < SMA200, or SMA200 below its value 21 days earlier.
IBD regime: distribution count ≥ 5 in the last 25 sessions, or the low of the latest rally attempt is undercut.
Breadth: % of universe with C > SMA200 < 40% (alt 50%).
Volatility: India VIX > 25 (alt 20/30; series starts 2008, so use 20d realized index vol > 25% annualized before that).
After a big index rally: Nifty 500 up > 30% in 126d, or > 15% above its SMA200 (expect lower breakout follow-through; hypothesis).
Sideways market: |SMA200 slope over 63d| < 2% with index within ±5% of SMA200 (expect whipsaws and failed breakouts).
Stock liquidity: median 20d turnover < ₹5 cr (alt ₹2/10 cr), or price < ₹20.
Late-stage base: base count ≥ 3, where the count resets when price undercuts the prior base low [B].
Base too deep vs market: base depth > 2 × the index drawdown over the same window [B].
Earnings within 5 trading days (needs an earnings-date file; not available from OHLCV).

4. Six backtest mistakes and fixes

Look-ahead in base detection. Swing highs and lows found with centered windows use future bars. Confirm a pivot only k bars after it prints, and compute RS percentiles only from data available that day.
Survivorship and today's constituents. Use point-in-time universes, including delisted names, and rebuild the liquidity filter daily.
Gaps and price bands through stops. Fill stops at min(open, stop). On locked limit-down days, carry the position to the next tradable open. On locked limit-up breakouts, assume no fill.
Costs and slippage. Model STT, exchange fees, stamp duty and GST (about 0.25% round trip for delivery) plus slippage of 0.1–0.5% scaled to turnover. Test at 2× costs too.
Overlapping trades and capital. Run a portfolio simulation with a cash constraint (for example 10–20 slots at 5–10% each, or 1% risk per trade). Rank same-day signals by RS. No re-entry while a position is open.
Curve-fitting. Fix the parameter grid before you start. Use walk-forward splits (2003–12 in-sample, 2013–19 validation, 2020–26 out-of-sample). Look for plateaus, not peaks, and report the number of trades per cell. Also use adjusted close for signals but raw prices for band and price filters, and make sure split/bonus adjustment doesn't create fake breakouts.

5. Independent test results

Bulkowski, cup with handle (REPORTED, US stocks): In bull markets the cup with handle ranks 3rd of 39 patterns, with a 5% break-even failure rate, a 54% average rise, and 61% of patterns reaching the price target (thepatternsite.com/cup.html). The rise is measured to the eventual peak, a "perfect trade" with hindsight, not a tradable exit. His older book edition using strict O'Neil rules reported only a 34% average rise (sponser.co.il PDF).
MarketInOut, Trend Template only (REPORTED): Rules were the 8 Trend Template criteria, exit on a close below the 200-day MA, 20 slots of 5% each, 0.1% commission, delisted stocks included. On all liquid BSE stocks since 2013 the strategy made 1.3% a year against 11.2% for a Sensex ETF, a 9.9-point annual shortfall. Across all liquid US stocks it returned 0.0% a year over 26 years, 8.2 points behind SPY. It did better on large, established stocks and worse on small ones. There was no NSE test, and it did not test VCP entries or tight stops (marketinout.com).
India momentum and liquidity (REPORTED): Over Dec 2006–Jun 2025 (BacktestIndia), the Momentum 30 portfolio had a 14.60% net CAGR against 10.41% for the Nifty 50. The less-liquid half made 19.43% and the more-liquid half 8.51%, with drawdowns of 66–75% (freefincal.com). Implication: expect much of any NSE edge to come from low-liquidity stocks, so model slippage carefully.
George & Hwang 2004 (CONFIRMED, peer-reviewed): How close a stock trades to its 52-week high explains a large share of momentum profits, beats past returns as a predictor of future returns, and those returns don't reverse in the long run (J. Finance 59(5)). A follow-up study found the strategy profitable in 18 of 20 international markets, significantly so in 10 (scribd copy). This is the academic support for the "within 25% of the high" rule.
CAN SLIM, Lutey et al. (REPORTED, small samples): A simplified screen beat the NASDAQ 100 by 0.94% a month from 2010 (Lutey, Crum, Rayome). A live paper-traded version from Jul 2014–Feb 2017 beat the S&P 500 by 20% (J. Accounting & Finance 22(2)). Both tested stock selection, not base breakouts.
Gaps: I found no peer-reviewed test of O'Neil bases or VCP breakouts on NSE.

Survivorship and hindsight in the books: The O'Neil "model book" and Minervini chart examples are winners picked after the fact. Base counts and "proper" handles are labelled with hindsight. Minervini's own results are self-reported and come from discretionary trading. Bulkowski's average rise is measured to the eventual peak. Test across all detected bases, never the curated examples.
