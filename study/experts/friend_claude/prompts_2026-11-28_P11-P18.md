## P11 — Fed के फ़ैसले लेने वाले + US रोज़गार के पहले आने वाले संकेत (gold की कड़ी)

CONTEXT: It is late November 2026. The FOMC meets 8-9 Dec 2026 and markets price a ~98% chance of a 25bp HIKE. Fed Chair is Kevin Warsh. October PCE was 3.8% y/y (core 3.3%) and Q3 GDP 4.3%. I study how gold (and Indian gold-linked stocks) moves 5-10 days ahead, through this chain: data → Fed expectations → real yields/dollar → gold.

PART A — PEOPLE (table): every 2026 FOMC voter and the non-voting presidents. Give name, role, voter in 2026/2027 (Y/N), hawk/dove score from -2 to +2, their last 3 public statements on rates (date, quote ≤20 words, link), how they voted at each 2026 meeting (dissents), who appointed them, and any political pressure on them (from the White House or Congress). Also cover the 2027 voter rotation: who becomes a voter in Jan 2027, and does that make the committee more hawkish or dovish?

PART B — LEADING INDICATORS of US jobs/inflation. List every indicator that tends to move BEFORE the official payrolls / unemployment / CPI numbers: weekly initial and continuing jobless claims, ADP, JOLTS openings and quits, ISM and S&P PMI employment sub-indices, Challenger layoffs, Indeed postings, WARN notices, Conference Board labour differential, NFIB hiring plans, temp-help jobs, gasoline and used-car prices for CPI, Cleveland Fed nowcast, and any others. For each give: release day/time, typical lead (in weeks), the published evidence on how well it predicts (correlations, studies, links), and a free data source (FRED series id where possible).

PART C — HISTORY: For 2004-2026, list FOMC meetings where the decision or statement SURPRISED the market (vs fed funds futures one day before). Give the date, expected vs actual, gold % move that day and the next 5 days, the DXY move, and the 2y yield move. Output as CSV: date,expected,actual,surprise_bp,gold_d0,gold_d5,dxy_d0,us2y_d0_bp,source.

PART D — The next 6 weeks of US data releases (calendar) with consensus where available.

OUTPUT: tables A and B as CSV, C as CSV, D as a list. Then a 10-line "how to forecast the next Fed move 2-4 weeks early" checklist.

## P12 — अफ़वाह के स्रोत का रिकॉर्ड (rumour-source scorecard)

ROLE: You are a financial-journalism researcher and event-study analyst. Use web search; give a link for every case.

GOAL: Learn which rumour sources are reliable. The case that started this: on 15 Nov 2026 the FT (unnamed sources) reported that Jensen Huang might return to Nvidia. On 16-17 Nov CNBC and the board chair denied it. On 23 Nov Nvidia's 8-K reinstated him.

TASK: Collect ≥60 cases (2010-2026), at least 25 of them Indian (ET, Mint, Moneycontrol, Bloomberg/Reuters on Indian companies, and exchange "clarification" filings where the company denied a media report). Each case is a market-moving report about a company or policy (M&A, CEO exit/return, stake sale, tax change, index change, SEBI action) that was based on unnamed or unofficial sources. For each case, give:
date_report, outlet, source_type (named / unnamed "people familiar" / social media / politician), n_sources_cited, company_or_policy, claim, denied (Y/N + date + who denied), outcome (true/false/partly) + date confirmed, days_to_outcome, stock move on day 0 and in the 5 days after the denial (if available), link_report, link_outcome.

Then summarise: the hit rate by outlet and by source type; how often a denial later turned out false; what typically predicts that a denied report is still true (e.g. "denial says 'speculative' instead of 'false'", the exact wording of Indian exchange clarifications, multiple outlets confirming independently). Give exact numbers.

OUTPUT: CSV + summary table + a 10-point checklist for judging a fresh rumour within the first hour.

Examples to check: time-series momentum, 52-week-high momentum, VWAP-based intraday, order-flow / open-interest buildup, market profile / value area, volatility-contraction, overnight vs intraday return split, post-earnings drift, short-term reversal, Indian F&O ban-period effects, circuit-limit magnet effect, block-deal and bulk-deal effects.
