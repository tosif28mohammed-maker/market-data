Linear Regression Indicators Explained | LSMA, Slope & LinReg Candles

In technical analysis, regression-based indicators help traders study trend direction, price movement and changes in momentum.

FAQs:

What is a Linear Regression Indicator?
A Linear Regression Indicator uses historical price data and a least squares calculation to estimate a fitted trend line over a selected period.

What is the Least Squares Method?
The Least Squares Method finds a line that minimises the sum of squared differences between observed prices and the values estimated by that line.

What is Linear Regression Curve?
Linear Regression Curve plots regression-based values across successive periods to help traders observe the underlying price trend.

What is Linear Regression Slope?
Linear Regression Slope measures the direction and steepness of a fitted regression line. Positive readings indicate an upward slope, while negative readings indicate a downward slope.

What is Least Squares Moving Average?
LSMA is a regression-based moving average that typically plots the endpoint of a least squares regression line calculated over a rolling price window.

What is the difference between LSMA and SMA?
SMA calculates the arithmetic average of prices over a period, while LSMA uses a fitted regression line to estimate its latest value.

What are Humble LinReg Candles?
Humble LinReg Candles is a chart indicator that applies linear regression calculations to candlestick price data to create a smoothed representation of price behaviour.

Can Linear Regression Candles be used for intraday trading?
Yes. They can be applied to intraday charts, including 1-minute and 5-minute timeframes, but smoothing does not eliminate false signals or trading risk.

Which is better: Linear Regression or Moving Average?
Neither is universally better. Regression-based indicators and traditional moving averages use different calculations and may respond differently to changing price conditions.
