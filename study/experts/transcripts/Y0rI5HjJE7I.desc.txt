A Decade After Brexit, Can Rejoining Europe Save Britain's Economy?

A decade after voting to leave the European Union, Britain is once again looking towards Europe. What was once sold as a historic opportunity to reclaim control over its economy, borders and laws has turned into a difficult economic and political reckoning. Now, the possibility of rebuilding ties with the EU — and even rejoining one day — is back in the conversation.

Brexit came with a significant economic price tag. Estimates suggest Britain's GDP per capita could be 6–8% lower by 2025 than it would have been had the country remained in the EU. Weaker trade, lower business investment, additional costs for businesses and rising food prices have all added to the debate over whether leaving Europe was worth it.

But can Britain actually go back?

Rejoining the European Union would not be straightforward. It would involve negotiations with all 27 member states, difficult questions over membership terms and potentially years of political and economic uncertainty. And even if Britain returned, could it recover the economic ground lost over the past decade?
