Belgium Protests: Why Thousands Are on Streets Against PM De Wever | Brussels Strike Explained

Why is Belgium protesting? Thousands of demonstrators marched through Brussels on Monday, November 23, 2026, as trade unions launched a nationwide strike against Prime Minister Bart De Wever's government's austerity measures, pension reforms and proposed spending cuts.

Around 50,000 protesters took to the streets, according to figures reported by Reuters, as workers voiced concerns over wages, pensions, healthcare spending, working conditions and the rising cost of living.

The nationwide strike brought major disruption to Belgium. Flights were cancelled across Belgian airports, while buses, trams and metro services faced severe interruptions, affecting thousands of commuters and air passengers.

The protests come amid a political and budget crisis. Prime Minister Bart De Wever's government is seeking approximately €10 billion in savings to reduce Belgium's budget deficit. However, disagreements over taxation, welfare and public spending have divided the governing coalition.
