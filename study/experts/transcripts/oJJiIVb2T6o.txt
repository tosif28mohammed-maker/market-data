On September 15, 2026, NPCI announced that charges will now apply on UPI transactions,
and the name given to this charge is MDR, i.e. Merchant Discount Rate, and these
charges will come into effect from October 15, 2026. Now the biggest
clarification in this is that any person-to-person transactions we
make — for instance, if I sent ₹5,000 to a friend, or sent money to
an aunt, uncle, or relative — whether it's ₹5,000, ₹15,000, or even
₹1 lakh — on such person-to-person transactions, no charge will apply.
This charge will apply only on merchant transactions. And when we buy
something, whether online or offline, we will not be the ones
bearing this charge. This charge will be borne by the merchant — meaning the shopkeeper
or the e-commerce platform will be the ones bearing that charge. And how much will this
charge be? It's 0.4% of the transaction value. For example, if you bought something
online for ₹10,000, 0.4% of that comes to how much? ₹40. This ₹40 will have
to be borne by the e-commerce platform. You don't have to pay it
out of your own pocket. Similarly, if you go to buy something from a shopkeeper's
store, in that case too you won't have to bear these charges. Besides that, there's
another limit — on transactions up to ₹2,000, there's no charge of any kind. Whether
you buy something online or buy something from a merchant, no
charge will apply there either. But above ₹2,000, even if it's just ₹1 more, then charges
of 0.4% will apply on the full ₹2,001. But after this, an upper cap has also
been set. No more than ₹300 transaction charge will apply in any case.
That means if we made a transaction of more than ₹75,000 — let's say a transaction of
₹1 lakh — even in that case, the merchant only has to pay a
