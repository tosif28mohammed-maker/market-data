FAQs:

What is Elliott Wave Theory?
Elliott Wave Theory is a technical analysis framework that studies recurring patterns of price advances and declines, often interpreted through impulsive and corrective wave structures.

Who developed Elliott Wave Theory?
Elliott Wave Theory was developed by Ralph Nelson Elliott.

What is an Elliott Wave Impulse Pattern?
An Elliott Wave impulse is generally represented by five waves labelled 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5, following specific structural rules.

What is a Corrective Wave?
A corrective wave is a price movement that generally moves against the larger trend and may form structures such as zigzags, flats and triangles.

How many waves are there in Elliott Wave Theory?
A commonly described complete cycle contains five motive waves followed by three corrective waves, although real-world structures can be more complex.

What are Elliott Wave Degrees?
Wave Degrees describe the relative size of wave patterns within a larger or smaller market structure. Examples include Minute, Minor, Intermediate, Primary, Cycle and Supercycle.

How do you draw Elliott Waves on charts?
Traders identify relevant swing points, apply a dedicated Elliott Wave drawing tool and label the waves according to their interpretation of the price structure.

Can Elliott Wave Theory predict stock prices?
Elliott Wave analysis can help traders develop possible market scenarios, but wave counts can be subjective and do not guarantee future price levels or timing.

What is the difference between Elliott Wave and Dow Theory?
Dow Theory focuses on identifying broader market trends and their confirmation, while Elliott Wave Theory examines recurring wave structures within market movements.

Is Elliott Wave the same as Wolfe Wave?
No. Elliott Wave Theory and Wolfe Wave patterns are different technical analysis approaches with different pattern structures.
