FAQs:

What is MACD?
MACD stands for Moving Average Convergence Divergence. It measures the relationship between two exponential moving averages to help traders analyse momentum and trend direction.

How does the MACD Indicator work?
Standard MACD is calculated by subtracting the 26-period EMA from the 12-period EMA. A 9-period EMA of the MACD line is commonly used as its signal line.

What is a MACD crossover?
A bullish crossover occurs when the MACD line moves above the signal line, while a bearish crossover occurs when it moves below. These are signals for analysis, not guaranteed trade outcomes.

What does the MACD Histogram show?
The MACD Histogram shows the difference between the MACD line and its signal line, helping visualise changes in their relationship.

What is CM MACD?
CM MACD is a modified MACD indicator that uses additional visual elements, including colour changes, to make momentum conditions and crossover signals easier to interpret.

What is a Price Oscillator?
A Price Oscillator measures the difference between moving averages of different periods. Depending on its calculation, the result may be expressed in price units or as a percentage.

What is Detrended Price Oscillator?
The Detrended Price Oscillator, or DPO, removes a longer-term trend component from price data to help highlight shorter-term price cycles.

Is DPO the same as MACD?
No. MACD measures the relationship between moving averages for trend and momentum analysis, while DPO focuses on price fluctuations after removing a trend component.

Which is better: MACD or Price Oscillator?
Neither is universally better. Their usefulness depends on the calculation, timeframe, market conditions and analytical objective.

Can MACD predict market reversals?
No. MACD can highlight momentum changes and potential trend transitions, but its signals may lag or fail.
