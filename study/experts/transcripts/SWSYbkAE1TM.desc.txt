Tata Steel & APL Apollo Shares Crash After ₹28,000 Cr Deal! Why?

Tata Steel and APL Apollo Tubes shares have fallen sharply after a massive ₹28,000 crore business deal. Why are investors worried about one of India's biggest steel-sector transactions?

Tata Steel has announced plans to acquire approximately 26.6% of APL Apollo Tubes' promoter stake and launch an additional 26% open offer. If the offer is fully accepted, Tata Steel could gain a controlling stake of approximately 52.38%.

At the same time, APL Apollo will acquire Tata Steel's steel tubes and colour-coated steel businesses for ₹28,000 crore.

So why are Tata Steel and APL Apollo share prices under pressure?

Here are 3 important things investors should understand:

1️⃣ Expensive Valuation: The acquired businesses are valued at an implied EV/EBITDA multiple of approximately 24.8x, raising concerns about the price being paid.

2️⃣ Debt and Financing Risks: APL Apollo plans to initially finance the transaction through acquisition funding and subsequently replace the debt with longer-term capital. Investors are watching the potential impact on borrowing costs and the balance sheet.

3️⃣ Execution Challenges: The combined business aims to expand capacity from approximately 7.5 million tonnes to 15.5 million tonnes by 2035. Achieving profitable growth at this scale will require significant execution.

Interestingly, APL Apollo's latest Q2 results showed strong operating performance, with revenue growth of 26.6%, EBITDA growth of 29.3%, and net profit growth of 26.8%.

Despite these results, the market is assessing whether the acquisition valuation, financing structure and long-term expansion plans justify the risks.

What should Tata Steel and APL Apollo investors monitor?

✅ Acquisition debt and interest costs

✅ Capacity utilisation and profitability of acquired assets

✅ Execution of the expansion plan through 2035

Will this deal create long-term value, or could financing and execution challenges affect shareholder returns?
