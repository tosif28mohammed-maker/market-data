Mutual Fund Returns Turn Negative! Should You Stop Your SIP?

Mutual Fund Returns 2026: Why are investors putting money into mutual funds even when recent returns are negative?

An analysis of India's 10 largest actively managed mutual fund schemes reveals a surprising trend. As of October 2026, eight of these schemes had delivered negative one-year returns. Yet seven attracted estimated net inflows during the month.

Together, these 10 schemes managed approximately ₹9.30 lakh crore in assets under management (AUM) and received an estimated ₹4,628 crore in net inflows during October 2026.

So where is this money going?

Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund:
One-year return: -6.06%
Estimated October net inflows: ₹1,137 crore
Three-year annualised return: 11.67%
Five-year annualised return: 10.06%

HDFC Flexi Cap Fund:
One-year return: -4.08%
Estimated October net inflows: ₹1,022 crore
Five-year annualised return: 14.76%

HDFC Mid Cap Fund:
One-year return: Approximately -4%
Estimated October net inflows: ₹998 crore

These three funds alone attracted approximately ₹3,157 crore, accounting for around 68% of the estimated net inflows across the selected 10 schemes.

What makes this trend interesting?

Although short-term performance has been weak, the selected schemes have positive three-year and five-year annualised returns.

However, positive net inflows do not necessarily mean investors are confident about future returns. SIP contributions, withdrawals and other transactions can occur simultaneously. Net flows only indicate the difference between money entering and leaving a fund.

Investors should also remember that these schemes belong to different mutual fund categories, including flexi-cap, mid-cap and hybrid funds.

Before selecting a mutual fund, consider these three factors:

1. Performance across multiple periods, not just the last 12 months.

2. Consistency against the scheme's appropriate benchmark and category peers.

3. Investment strategy, portfolio risk and suitability for your financial goals.
