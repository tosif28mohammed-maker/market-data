Before taking a loan: How to borrow responsibly

Before taking any loan, borrowers should shift their focus from eligibility to affordability and assess whether borrowing is truly necessary. A loan is not extra income but an advance on future earnings that carries interest, meaning a lower monthly EMI often masks a higher total repayment cost due to an extended tenure. To understand the true cost of borrowing, evaluate the Annual Percentage Rate (APR), which combines interest with processing fees and other charges into a single annualised figure. For retail borrowers, regulated lenders must provide this information transparently in a Key Facts Statement before an agreement is signed. While high-interest unsecured options like personal loans are common, individuals with investments can consider secured alternatives like a loan against securities (LAS). This option allows borrowers to pledge eligible shares or mutual funds for liquidity at a lower interest rate without selling their assets, though it requires careful management of market-driven loan-to-value ratios to avoid forced collateral liquidation.
