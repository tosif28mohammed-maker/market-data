## Point-by-point
- Wealth Creation Study 2010-2015 highlights that Top 100 wealth creators generated INR 34.2 trillion wealth, with TCS, ITC, and HDFC Bank leading the list.
- Fastest wealth creators (led by Ajanta Pharma, Symphony, Eicher Motors) had small base market caps (< INR 20 Billion in 2010) and saw massive P/E re-rating driven by hyper earnings growth.
- Most consistent wealth creator over 2005-2015 was Titan (43% 10-year price CAGR). 8 out of top 10 consistent wealth creators are consumer-facing companies.
- Markets track earnings growth closely: 10-year PAT CAGR and Price CAGR correlation is 0.7. Over 2010-15, Wealthex index generated 25% CAGR vs Sensex 10% CAGR, driven by 19% PAT CAGR vs 10% Sensex PAT CAGR.
- Value migration is a core driver: capital migrating from PSUs to Private Banks, and global demand migrating to Indian IT and Healthcare.
- PSU wealth creation hit an all-time low (only 5 PSUs in top 100, creating just 2% of total wealth).
- Young companies (age 1-20 years) and small cap companies (< INR 50 billion market cap) delivered the highest price CAGR (45% vs 25% market average).
- Global commodity companies (Metals/Mining) and PSUs dominated the top wealth destroyers list (INR 14.6 trillion wealth destroyed, led by MMTC, RIL, SAIL, NMDC, BHEL).
- Historical Study 2 (1992-1997): Wealth creators exhibit rising PAT margin, stable asset turnover, and falling debt gearing, with RoCE higher than prevailing coupon rates.
- Historical Study 9 (1999-2004): Commodity cycles move through Gloom -> Recovery -> Squeeze -> Euphoria -> Glut. Investors must 'sell too soon' during the Squeeze phase.
- Historical Study 11 (2001-2006): Terms of Trade measured as Debtors / Creditors. Favorable terms (Debtors < Creditors) indicate high supplier/customer bargaining power.
- Historical Study 14 (2004-2009): Winner categories are sectors growing at >= 1.5x nominal GDP growth.
