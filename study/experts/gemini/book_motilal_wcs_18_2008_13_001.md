# Motilal Oswal 18th Annual Wealth Creation Study (2008-2013) Key Findings
स्रोत: book: motilal_wcs_18_2008_13.txt · motilal_wcs_18_2008_13.txt · Gemini ने पढ़ा 2026-11-23 14:40 UTC

## Point-by-point
- Technology sector emerged as a prime candidate to become India's largest wealth creator due to realistic valuation (19x P/E vs market 15x) compared to Consumer sector (33x P/E, >2x market average leaving no re-rating room).
- PSUs saw unprecedented underperformance, dropping to an all-time low of 11 companies in the top 100 wealth creators, making up only 9% of total wealth created, with total absence in Utilities, Metals & Mining, and Capital Goods.
- Company age is agnostic to stock price CAGR (hovers around 17-19% across all age groups), whereas smaller base market cap (< INR 5 billion / Rs 500 Cr) exhibits strong inverse correlation with returns, delivering up to 65% Price CAGR.
- High earnings power (RoE > 30%) acts as an economic moat, delivering higher multi-year stock returns (21-24% Price CAGR) even with moderate PAT growth (14-15%), outperforming low RoE (<15%) stocks despite high PAT growth (36% PAT CAGR).
- Payback Ratio < 1x (Market Cap <= 5-Year Cumulative Profits) proved to be the single most reliable valuation metric across market cycles, yielding 24% Price CAGR.
