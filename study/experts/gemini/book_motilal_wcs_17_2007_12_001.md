## Point-by-point
- Economic Moat Period (EMP) testing criteria requires minimum 8 to 10 years of financial history and Market Cap >= INR 500 million (Rs 50 Crore).
- 8-Year Economic Moat Rule: A company qualifies as an Economic Moat Company (EMC) if its RoE is higher than the industry average RoE for at least 6 out of 8 years (1995-2002).
- 10-Year Economic Moat Rule: For a 10-year evaluation period (2002-2012), the threshold is raised to RoE exceeding industry average in at least 7 out of 10 years.
- Consumer sector exception: Consumer companies with high absolute RoE lacking direct peer groups are automatically classified as EMCs.
- Exclusion rule: If RoE trend shows a clearly broken business model closer to the investment date, the stock is disqualified as the moat is drying up.
- Backtest results show EMCs achieved 25% CAGR over 2003-2012 vs Non-EMCs 12% CAGR vs Sensex benchmark 18% CAGR.
- Non-EMC Upgrade Payoff: Non-EMCs that upgraded to EMCs (N-Y) generated the highest 10-year compounded returns of 27% CAGR (with a 25% transition rate).
- EMC Status Quo: 84% of EMCs maintained their moat status (Y-Y) delivering 26% CAGR, while fallen EMCs (Y-N) still delivered 20% CAGR outperforming non-EMCs.
- Market Outlook Macro Benchmark - Corporate Profit to GDP: 10-year historical average is 4.6%. Values around 4.5%-5.0% indicate normal valuation cycles.
- Market Outlook Macro Benchmark - Sensex P/E: 10-year historical average Sensex P/E is 14.8x. Forward P/E of ~14.4x represents reasonable long-term valuation.
- Market Outlook Macro Benchmark - Earnings Yield to Bond Yield: 15-year average ratio is 0.92x. A ratio around 0.9x indicates equity yields are near parity with bond yields.
- Market Outlook Macro Benchmark - Market Cap to GDP: Long-term average is 50%, while peak bull market levels reach 103% to 115%.
